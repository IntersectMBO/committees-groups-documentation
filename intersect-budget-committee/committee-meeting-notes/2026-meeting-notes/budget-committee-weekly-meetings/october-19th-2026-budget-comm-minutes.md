## Discussion/Actions

**Quorum:** Confirmed

### 1. Opening

Simo Simovic opened the meeting and confirmed quorum.

The main agenda item was review of the draft charter for a proposed working group focused on treasury administration, transparency, and delivery assurance.

### 2. Draft Working Group Charter

Nicolas Cerny presented the draft charter prepared following the previous meeting.

The proposed working group would review how Intersect administers Treasury Withdrawals and produce practical recommendations that could be used to improve administrative services.

The draft proposed three main stages:

* Review of the current process and status quo.
* Stakeholder engagement and comparison with relevant best practices.
* Development of recommendations and handover.

Members noted that there is now sufficient experience and historical data from funded proposals and milestone delivery to review what is working well and what could be improved.

### 3. Access to Information and Intersect Support

Members discussed whether the proposed review could be completed using publicly available information or whether cooperation from Intersect's administration and delivery assurance teams would be required.

Concerns were raised that some relevant information may not currently be public and that the committee should understand whether Intersect would support the review and provide access to necessary information.

Members also questioned whether recommendations produced by the working group would be considered and potentially implemented.

There was agreement that the committee should seek alignment with the Intersect Board and relevant internal teams before committing significant time to the initiative.

### 4. Scope of the Proposed Work

The committee discussed whether the draft charter had become too focused on reviewing Intersect's administration process compared with the broader treasury transparency discussion from the previous meeting.

The earlier discussion had focused more broadly on making Treasury Withdrawal information easier to find and understand, including funding status, milestone delivery, and reporting across different administrators.

The current draft intentionally focuses first on Intersect's administration framework because it covers the majority of funded proposals and could provide standards or lessons that other administrators might later adopt.

Members agreed that the scope needs to be clearly defined before proceeding.

### 5. Authority, Capacity and Funding

Members discussed whether the committee has sufficient authority and capacity to undertake the proposed work.

Several members noted that the review could require substantial time and should not rely entirely on informal volunteer effort if the expectation is to produce a meaningful and professional result.

The possibility of funding or compensation for working group participants was raised.

Members also emphasized the importance of confirming that the Board supports the work and that recommendations would have a realistic path toward implementation.

### 6. Budget Committee Priorities

The discussion broadened into the overall role and priorities of the Budget Committee.

Members acknowledged that the committee has discussed several important topics since completing the 2026 Budget Process retrospective, including:

* Treasury accountability after funding.
* Transparency and monitoring.
* KPI tracking.
* DRep workload and expert review.
* Proposal quality and evaluation standards.
* Future budget process improvements.

However, concern was raised that these discussions have not yet resulted in clear deliverables.

Members reflected that the committee had a strong role in developing the previous Budget Process framework, but that its current priorities and ownership for the remainder of 2026 are less clear.

There was broad agreement that the committee needs to move from discussion toward a smaller number of concrete priorities and outputs.

### 7. Problem Definition and Working Group Approach

Rodrigo Pacini suggested that before approving the working group, the committee should first document the main problems it believes need to be addressed and determine which of them are shared priorities.

The intent would be to ensure that the proposed working group is solving clearly understood problems and that there is sufficient member support and capacity to deliver the work.

Others cautioned that an extended problem-sensing exercise could lead to further discussion without action and potentially broaden the scope even further.

One suggested approach was to develop alternative proposals or charters and allow the committee to decide between them rather than attempting to reach full consensus on every issue.

No decision was made to formally establish the working group during the meeting.

### 8. Potential Narrower Deliverables

The committee discussed whether the initiative could be reduced to a narrower treasury accountability workstream with a limited number of concrete outputs.

Potential outputs discussed included:

* A common reporting structure for treasury-funded initiatives.
* A pilot using a small number of existing Treasury Withdrawals.
* Recommendations on who should own or maintain the process going forward.

Members agreed that any proposed work should have a clear problem statement, achievable scope, identified ownership, and realistic expectations regarding the time required.

### 9. Actions and Next Steps

* Committee members to review the draft working group charter and provide direct feedback or edits.
* Further define the problems the committee wants to address before approving the working group.
* Consider narrowing the scope into a smaller number of concrete deliverables.
* Assess committee member capacity and willingness to participate.
* Prepare a clearer proposal before raising the initiative with the Intersect Board and relevant teams.
* Continue discussion asynchronously via Discord before the next meeting.
