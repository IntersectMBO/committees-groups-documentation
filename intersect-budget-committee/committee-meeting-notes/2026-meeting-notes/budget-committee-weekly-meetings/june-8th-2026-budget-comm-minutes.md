#### 5.2 The ADA-Price / Milestone Problem

A significant portion of the discussion addressed ADA price volatility undermining funded budgets (proposals submitted at \~25–30 cents; ADA now \~16 cents — a \~40–50% effective cut).

* **Nico:** Raised a downside-management gap the Civics Committee may ask the Budget Committee's opinion on. Example: the IO research proposal expired today, budgeted at 25 cents/ADA, now \~16 cents. The proposal contains **no language managing that downside**. **Intersect as administrator must ensure proposals uphold their end of the bargain; if they can't, the money should return to the treasury.** What must **not** happen is a quiet **rearrangement/downgrade of milestones between administrator and proposer** — that would violate community governance.
* **Can ("John") Huzmeli:** Explained the smart-contract mechanics. The administrator's only options are: (a) **stay in ADA** — same ADA, same milestones; or (b) **match to USDC**, set to the ADA amount on the **day of signature** (so if ADA is \~15 cents at signing, that's the USDC equivalent). **The risk sits entirely with the company, not Intersect.** The practical consequence: teams facing \~40% less cash flow than planned may simply **cancel their contracts** rather than deliver at a loss.
* **Christina:** Vehemently opposed letting proposers **redefine milestones** because the price moved — funds should be **returned** and a new proposal resubmitted at current rates; the community voted for specific outcomes. Noted many proposals included **contingency funds** intended to offset exactly this, though some DReps have argued (questionably) that using them this way isn't appropriate.
* **Stephen Wood:** Argued ADA is **too volatile to budget in** — a speculative asset, not money in the traditional sense. A fundamental fix worth considering: denominate/pay in **stablecoins**. Forcing proposers to pick an ADA price and pack in contingencies defeats the purpose of a budget.
* **Kristijan:** DReps won't approve **top-ups for price drops** — that's unreasonable. Pointed to a **systemic need for faster, more agile governance**; current processes take weeks. Real-world stakes are severe — builders are closing shops; on a Friday call, developers without work were asking for any incoming opportunities.

#### 5.3 Ecosystem Maturity and Accountability

* **Christina:** Cited **Tap Tools'** shutdown announcement despite receiving \~**$500K from CBD DAO (in USD) over \~9 months** (including \~$150K recently) — raising questions about overhead and management.
* **Lloyd:** These are privately held, often very young entities in an immature ecosystem lacking the delivery-and-solvency proof requirements common in government contracting. Compared it to the dot-com era (where only Google survived the perks-driven excess). Tap Tools also suffered a **brain drain** — technical staff left — and the wider ecosystem is losing talent to better-paying AI/other work amid high friction.
* **Christina:** Suggested funded teams may need **business-leadership training** — a possible future initiative, with stronger requirements on funded teams.
* **Nico:** Framed it as a crypto-wide problem — ecosystems "subsidize the hell out of" projects, and many teams learn to **farm grants** rather than build sustainable business models. Predicted a **"great washout" in 2026** that clears out funding-seekers, leaving dedicated teams genuinely building toward revenue — the ones worth supporting (versus "600K ADA for a calendar" or "1M ADA for a dashboard").
* **Lloyd:** Reflected on the broader financialization of open ecosystems, and committed to keep grappling with it — with or without funding.
