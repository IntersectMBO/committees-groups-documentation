# October 5th, 2026 Budget Comm Minutes

**Budget Committee Weekly Meeting — October 5th, 2026**

**Date:** October 5, 2026\
**Time:** 13:00 UTC

**Attendees:** Megan Hess (Chair), Eric Helms (SCATDAO), Fanny Wijaya, Nicolas Cerny, Rodrigo Pacini, Christina Gianelloni (SME), Simo Simovic (Acting Secretary)

**Quorum:** Confirmed (4 of 8 voting members present)

### 1. Opening

Simo Simovic opened the meeting and confirmed quorum.

The main agenda items were the status of two inactive committee members, an update on the 2027 Budget Process, and discussion on potential Q4 work around treasury transparency, proposal monitoring, and the use of domain experts in future budget processes.

### 2. Inactive Committee Members

The committee reviewed the status of Otavio Lima and Jose Velasquez, who had not attended Budget Committee meetings during the previous two months.

The relevant dismissal and replacement policy was reviewed. It was noted that committee members may be subject to dismissal where attendance or participation falls below the thresholds defined in the policy.

A motion was raised to dismiss both members and was seconded by the Chair.

Because the affected members were not present, the committee agreed to follow the policy requirement to notify them and provide an opportunity to respond before a formal vote is held.

It was also clarified that removing the two members would leave the committee with eight voting members, so immediate replacement would not be required under the current policy.

### 3. 2027 Budget Process Update

An update was provided on ongoing discussions regarding the 2027 Budget Process.

Intersect is continuing discussions with major ecosystem stakeholders to determine the overall direction and whether a domain- or category-based approach should be developed further.

The current thinking is to introduce more structure into treasury spending by connecting domains or categories with clear goals and KPIs.

Before developing the detailed process, the approach will need to be socialized with DReps and other major stakeholders to understand whether there is sufficient support to proceed.

Further updates are expected as these discussions progress.

### 4. Treasury Transparency and Monitoring

The committee discussed a potential Q4 initiative to improve visibility into the status and progress of treasury-funded initiatives.

Members noted that information on funded proposals is currently spread across multiple platforms and sources, particularly for projects that are not administered through Intersect.

A proposal was made to consolidate information on how different Treasury Withdrawals are being monitored, including where progress reports, milestone information, and funding data can be found.

The initial idea is to create a structured overview that could potentially be published as a blog post or other public resource, making it easier for the community to understand the status of funded initiatives.

Nicolas Cerny agreed to prepare an initial outline, with Christina Gianelloni supporting the work.

### 5. Domain Experts and Conflicts of Interest

The committee discussed the potential use of domain experts within a more structured 2027 Budget Process.

Members acknowledged that specialist expertise could reduce the burden on DReps and improve the quality of proposal assessment. However, concerns were raised around conflicts of interest, particularly where the most knowledgeable subject matter experts may also be involved in companies or projects seeking treasury funding.

The committee agreed that strong safeguards would be required, including clear selection criteria, transparency, conflict-of-interest rules, and appropriate oversight mechanisms.

Members emphasized that any system designed to filter, assess, or recommend proposals must maintain legitimacy and avoid creating the perception that a small group is controlling access to treasury funding.

### 6. Oversight and Risk Management

The committee discussed the broader governance risks associated with a domain-based funding model.

Members suggested that a useful starting point would be to identify the main risks associated with using domain experts or other intermediary assessment mechanisms before designing safeguards.

Potential areas of concern include conflicts of interest, concentration of decision-making, lack of independence, and the possibility of expert recommendations unduly influencing DRep decisions.

The committee agreed that some form of risk register or structured risk assessment could help guide future process design.
