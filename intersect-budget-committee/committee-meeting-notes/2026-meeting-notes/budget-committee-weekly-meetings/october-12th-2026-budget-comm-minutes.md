## Discussion/Actions

**Quorum:** Confirmed

### 1. Opening

Simo Simovic opened the meeting and confirmed quorum.

The main agenda items were the formal dismissal vote for two inactive committee members and continuation of the discussion on treasury transparency, monitoring, and a potential Budget Committee working group.

It was also confirmed that Eric Helms agreed to extend his committee term until April 2027, when the next committee election is expected to take place.

### 2. Committee Member Dismissal Vote

The committee reviewed the attendance report prepared for Otávio Lima and Jose Velasquez following the motion raised at the October 5 meeting.

Both members had been formally notified of the motion and given an opportunity to provide context before the vote.

Jose Velasquez responded that ongoing conflicts with his regular working hours prevented him from attending meetings and indicated that he was comfortable with being removed from the committee.

Otávio Lima attended the meeting and provided context regarding personal and family circumstances that had prevented him from participating during August and September.

The committee proceeded with separate votes for each member.

For **Jose Velasquez**, all five voting members participating in the vote supported dismissal. The motion therefore passed.

For **Otávio Lima**, five members voted in favour of dismissal and one abstained. The motion therefore passed.

Both members were formally removed from their Budget Committee voting seats.

The committee confirmed that immediate replacement is not required, as eight voting members remain. The vacant seats are expected to be filled through the next committee election in April 2027.

### 3. Treasury Transparency and Monitoring

The committee continued the discussion started at the previous meeting on improving visibility into treasury-funded initiatives.

An initial overview had been prepared covering different Treasury Withdrawals, their administration models, available reporting sources, and current status.

The discussion highlighted that treasury-funded initiatives are currently administered through several different models, including Intersect-managed smart contracts, other Intersect-administered arrangements, self-administered projects, and separate funding structures.

Members agreed that the information needed to understand the status of funded initiatives exists, but is fragmented across multiple platforms, dashboards, websites, repositories, and reporting systems.

This makes it difficult for DReps and the wider community to obtain a comprehensive view of what has been funded, how much has been withdrawn, what remains available, and whether milestones have been completed.

### 4. Improving Accessibility of Treasury Information

The committee discussed creating a more comprehensive public resource that consolidates information about treasury-funded initiatives.

Potential information to include could cover:

* Treasury Withdrawal and funding details.
* Amount withdrawn and remaining.
* Current project status.
* Milestone and delivery information.
* Links to relevant smart contracts, dashboards, reports, repositories, and other supporting evidence.

Members discussed whether this should initially be a simple public page or structured dataset rather than a complex new dashboard.

There was also agreement that funded projects should be encouraged to provide clearer and more consistent public information, particularly where they are not administered through Intersect.

### 5. Delivery Assurance and Transparency

The discussion expanded beyond simply aggregating information to the wider delivery assurance process.

Members raised concerns that public information does not always provide enough context to understand why milestones were accepted or rejected, what evidence was reviewed, or how delivery decisions were reached.

It was noted that improving transparency may therefore require both better tooling and clearer standards around what information should be publicly available.

The committee discussed the possibility of reviewing the current Intersect delivery assurance process and providing recommendations on areas such as:

* Transparency of milestone reviews.
* Availability and quality of evidence.
* Traceability of milestone decisions.
* Consistency of reporting.
* Accessibility of information for DReps and the wider community.
* Experience of vendors going through the administration process.

Members emphasized that the Budget Committee would not take ownership of the delivery assurance function, but could assess the current public process and provide recommendations to the relevant Intersect team.

### 6. Potential Treasury Transparency Working Group

Members discussed whether the work should be progressed through a dedicated working group.

There was general support for the working group approach because it would allow interested Budget Committee members to work with additional community participants and relevant stakeholders rather than requiring the full committee to meet more frequently.

Members emphasized that any working group should have a clearly defined scope, objective, and deliverable rather than existing indefinitely.

Potential outputs discussed included:

* A consolidated overview of current Treasury Withdrawals and their status.
* An assessment of the current delivery assurance and transparency process.
* Recommendations for improving reporting, traceability, and community visibility.
* A public KB page, report, or similar resource summarizing the findings.

Before formally creating the working group, the committee agreed that its proposed remit and expected outputs should first be documented and approved.

### 7. Actions and Next Steps

* Remove Jose Velasquez and Otávio Lima from Budget Committee voting membership following the approved dismissal motions.
* Maintain eight voting members until the next committee election in April 2027 unless replacement becomes necessary under the policy.
* Continue developing the consolidated overview of Treasury Withdrawals and available monitoring information.
* Prepare a draft remit and proposed deliverables for a Treasury Transparency working group.
* Review the draft working group scope at the next Budget Committee meeting before deciding whether to formally establish it.
* Consider engaging Intersect's delivery assurance team, DReps, vendors, and other relevant stakeholders as part of the assessment.
* Share the draft working group proposal with the committee ahead of the next meeting.
