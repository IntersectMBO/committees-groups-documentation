### Marketing and Go-to-Market Benchmarks

#### Growth and Scale-Up Organizations

Organizations in growth and scale-up phases typically allocate a larger share of resources to market development. Marketing expenditure is commonly estimated at between 15% and 30% of revenue during these phases.

Marketing, however, represents only part of what this guide defines as growth. Business development, communications, events, community, onboarding, and ecosystem execution more closely resemble the broader corporate concept of go-to-market.

Technology and software companies may allocate between 30% and 50% of revenue to combined sales and marketing during growth phases, with expenditure tending toward approximately one-third of revenue as organizations mature.

Companies that are still validating markets and developing distribution often invest more as a share of revenue rather than less.

#### **Enterprise Adoption and Cumulative Touchpoints**

Enterprise adoption is rarely produced by a single event, campaign, or business development conversation. It develops through repeated interactions across multiple functions, including:

* Communications and positioning,
* Events and relationship building,
* Business development,
* Technical validation,
* Product and integration support,
* Onboarding,
* Operational execution,
* Continued engagement after initial contact.

Enterprise sales and marketing cycles may extend over 12 to 36 months and involve more than 60 touchpoints before a purchasing or deployment decision.

The value of an event such as the Cardano Summit should therefore not be assessed only through attendance, media exposure, or immediate conversions. Its contribution depends on whether it creates qualified relationships and whether those relationships are subsequently supported through business development, technical engagement, onboarding, and execution.

Events, communications, and business development should be understood as connected touchpoints within a wider adoption journey rather than as isolated activities.

This reinforces an important evaluation principle: Market-facing activity creates value when it advances relationships, opportunities, or users toward measurable adoption.

#### Market Trends and External Context

The market environment in which blockchain ecosystems compete continues to evolve rapidly.

Gartner identified digital provenance as one of its strategic technology trends for 2026, reflecting enterprise demand to verify the origin and integrity of code, data, processes, and media.

Gartner data presented at its 2025 IT Symposium also indicated that 84% of surveyed enterprises had increased generative AI budgets, with an average reported increase of 37.9%.

These trends illustrate that organizations are allocating resources selectively toward technologies and outcomes that can be connected to business value, operational improvement, or risk reduction.

Gartner’s blockchain outlook places the technology within a longer path from experimentation and disillusionment toward broader productivity around 2030. Its enterprise framing emphasizes that organizations do not generally purchase blockchain as an abstract technology. They seek solutions to specific problems involving:

* Growth,
* Cost,
* Risk,
* Trust,
* Provenance,
* Operational efficiency.

Blockchain becomes relevant when its capabilities are connected to those needs within specific markets and verticals.

For Cardano, this means that technical capability must be translated into clear use cases, credible positioning, appropriate partnerships, and executable adoption paths.

#### Cardano and Growth Investment

The original analysis estimated that the Cardano Treasury allocated approximately US$6 million to marketing from a total budget of approximately US$130 million.

This figure was used to argue that Cardano’s marketing investment was below common corporate benchmarks. However, this comparison should be treated cautiously because:

* Treasury budgets are not equivalent to company revenue;
* Marketing classifications may exclude business development, events, community, and ecosystem execution;
* Cardano-related growth expenditure may be distributed across several organizations and funding mechanisms;
* Spending levels alone do not indicate effectiveness.

The more useful question is not whether Cardano matches a corporate percentage, but whether its combined growth expenditure is sufficient, coordinated, and connected to measurable adoption outcomes.

#### Comparative Ecosystem Scale and Spend

Comparable spending data across blockchain ecosystems remains limited. Most major layer 1 foundations do not report marketing and growth as a consistent financial category, and several publish little or no detailed expenditure data.

Public metrics such as total value secured or DeFi TVL provide some indication of ecosystem scale, but they do not establish causality between spending and adoption.

**Interpretation**

The comparison demonstrates the difficulty of deriving spending recommendations from other ecosystems.

Polkadot, for example, disclosed substantial marketing and outreach expenditure during 2024, yet expenditure alone does not establish whether adoption outcomes improved proportionately. Likewise, ecosystems with stronger activity metrics may not disclose how much they spend on growth or how their market-facing functions are organized.

The relevant test is therefore not the absolute size of a growth budget but whether expenditure:

* Addresses a clearly identified ecosystem bottleneck,
* Connects to a credible adoption journey,
* Produces measurable progress,
* Creates durable value,
* Generates evidence that improves future decisions.

### Key Conclusions

The external evidence supports five conclusions:

1. Technical capability does not automatically produce adoption.
2. Organizations seeking growth invest in multiple connected market-facing functions, not just technical development.
3. Enterprise adoption requires sustained engagement across multiple touchpoints and functions.
4. External spending benchmarks can provide context, but they cannot determine how much Cardano should spend.

The appropriate governance question is whether the expenditure contributes credibly to sustainable adoption and generates evidence for better future decisions.
