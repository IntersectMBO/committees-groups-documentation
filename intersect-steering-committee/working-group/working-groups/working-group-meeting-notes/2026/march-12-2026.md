| Policy Timelines & Alignment                     | <p>- The group reviewed the rollout strategy for the core Displacement &#x26; Dismissal Policy updates.</p><p>- Lorenzo noted that the Intersect Steering Committee (ISC) requested this update alongside the transition to 2-year committee terms.</p><p>- Gintama urged the group to complete drafts within two weeks to shift focus toward optimizing the business process models before the next cohorts take office.</p>                                                                                                                                                                            | Lorenzo: Consolidate the working draft to present to the ISC for a preliminary review on Monday week.                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Working Group Socialization & Echo Chamber Risks | <p>- Terence (Tax) flagged a systemic risk that the working group functions as an echo chamber of the same 4–5 participants, lacking broader community input before passing policies.</p><p>- Gintama counter-argued that a lack of intrinsic reward or incentive limits general ecosystem interest in lengthy, asynchronous policy-drafting work.</p><p>- The group discussed that over 70+ members currently sit across all committees and representing their collective feedback is a critical first step.</p>                                                                                        | All Committee Chairs: Tasked with explicitly bringing the upcoming policy drafts back to their respective committees to secure a 10x multiplier on direct feedback data.Lorenzo: Promote the working group visibility publicly as soon as the revised working group directory tools go live. |
| Phased Escalation vs. Immediate Dismissal        | <p>- The group refined the graduation of penalties to avoid jumping straight to a harsh dismissal process.</p><p>- Lorenzo reiterated that stopping monthly compensation serves as a clean, low-level tracking point for non-participation.</p><p>- Gintama proposed that missing a meeting without a valid excuse should temporarily stall a member's voting rights for the subsequent call, since they would lack contextual awareness.</p>                                                                                                                                                            | Working Group: Structure the exact sequence of the phased process (Reward Pause → Vote Suspension → Formal Accountability Review) in the main document draft.                                                                                                                                |
| Defining Participation Benchmarks                | <p>- Gintama suggested a flat 50% monthly meeting attendance benchmark to stop compensation, noting that committees with low workloads meet as rarely as twice a month.</p><p>- Thiago pushed for a stricter 75% minimum voting participation rate on asynchronous committee business via the internal portals.</p><p>- The group noted that calculating hard hourly mandates is impractical since active groups like Budget meet multiple times a week while others operate on a bi-weekly maintenance schedule.</p>                                                                                    | Lorenzo: Frame the criteria around a combination of percentage-based meeting attendance and mandatory asynchronous vote counts.                                                                                                                                                              |
| Value-Driven Incentives vs. Punishment           | <p>- Jose and Mark argued that the group is overly focused on penalizing low attendance rather than building production-focused frameworks (such as milestone-based sprints or backlogs) that make members feel seen.</p><p>- Jose shared that the Cardano Foundation Ambassador Program utilizes fixed dollar-pegged compensation tied to verifiable proof of public value contribution.</p><p>- Jose proposed aligning with CF stakeholders (Alexander Maza and Sebastian Ravena) to potentially tie Project Catalyst funding application permissions to active Intersect membership registration.</p> | Jose: Coordinate an initial alignment call with CF representatives to explore shared community onboarding and incentive milestones.                                                                                                                                                          |
| Strict Enforcement Rules & Outreach Mandates     | <p>- Terence strongly asserted that no dismissal should ever be automatic or purely script-driven unless an explicit, multi-channel outreach protocol fails.</p><p>- A strict removal can only trigger if a member ignores a sequential wave of notifications spanning a 4-week window across four distinct channels: public Discord tags, direct messages, internal committee chat pings, and direct emails.</p>                                                                                                                                                                                        | Lorenzo: Codify the 4-channel outreach sequence directly into the operational guidelines of the dismissal policy text to safeguard against unfair removals.                                                                                                                                  |
